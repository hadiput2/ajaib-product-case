Operating model

How partner account opening works.

Ajaib has established several B2B partnerships with banks and other fintech companies. Partners own the initial KYC collection after account binding, pass that data to Ajaib and then follow one of two account-opening models.

1. Account bindingThe customer begins through the partner.
2. Partner collects initial KYCThe partner collects the customer’s KYC data.
3. Partner sends KYC data to AjaibAjaib receives it for processing.

The flow now splits based on who opens the account.

Model A · Partner opens

4A. Ajaib completes necessary KYCFor example, government investment ID creation.
5A. Information returns to the partner
6A. Partner opens the account

Model B · Ajaib opens

4B. Ajaib completes necessary KYCAny additional required process happens on Ajaib’s side.
5B. Ajaib opens the account directly

Shared outcome: once the account is open, the customer can trade in the Ajaib app.

02 · Diagnosis

Conversion tells us how many. Latency tells us how long.

The funnel shows where performance changed, but it doesn’t yet explain why. Before committing the squad to a fix, we need to test timing, processing delays and customer mix, so we can separate a genuine journey failure from a measurement or audience-mix effect.

Observed signal
Approval-to-deposit deteriorated most.

The decline is 17.6 percentage points, compared with 9.0 points at KYC submission and 2.1 points at approval. That makes post-approval the first place to investigate, but it does not prove that the underlying problem starts there.

Case clock

The 14-day clock starts at registration.

When approval happens later, customers have fewer observed days left to make a deposit. This means conditional conversion can fall even when post-approval behavior has not changed.

Competing explanation

Did the customer mix change?

We should compare partners, campaigns, acquisition promises and customer intent. A larger share of lower-intent customers could change the funnel even if the product is working as designed.

Decision principle

Follow the evidence upstream when timing explains the loss.

If approval has slowed but conversion remains stable when customers are compared at the same point after approval, the investigation should move upstream before we assign the squad.

03 · Partner

One customer problem. Explicit decision rights.

The first step with the partner is to agree on one version of the data and one view of the customer problem. From there, the team that controls the failure point should own the next action, with leadership escalation where needed.

Diagnose the correct partnership model

Case fact

The case is clear that partners collect the initial KYC after account binding and pass the data to Ajaib. The variable is who opens the account. An Ajaib-owned initial KYC collection flow would therefore be a new hypothesis, not a capability established by the prompt.

MODEL A · PARTNER OPENSPartner collects → Ajaib processes → partner opensAjaib completes the additional KYC and returns the information the partner needs to open the account.
MODEL B · AJAIB OPENSPartner collects → Ajaib processes → Ajaib opensAccount readiness, failure ownership and the customer handoff may therefore differ from Model A.

Because these two operating models can fail in different places, we should segment them first and diagnose each journey separately.

06 · Operating model

Move fast, without compromising customer trust.

My role here is to decide where the squad can have the most impact, be explicit about any commitment we may need to delay and make sure everyone knows who has the final call on release readiness.

When should we change an existing commitment?

Case constraint
My initial recommendationRedirect one squad now.

Use the first 48 hours to validate the mechanism, contain customer impact and select the intervention with the strongest evidence. We should change another commitment only if added capacity would materially improve the outcome.

Stay with one squadStay with the current squad when customer exposure is contained and that squad owns the critical path.
Change a commitmentAdd capacity when customer, compliance or account-integrity risk cannot be contained.
Recoverable impactPrioritize additional capacity only when the validated mechanism affects substantial recoverable volume.
Real accelerationBring in another squad when the work can run in parallel and added capacity will materially shorten recovery.
Opportunity costMake the trade-off only when the expected recovery and avoided risk outweigh the value of the commitment we would delay.
