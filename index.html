02 · Diagnosis

We need to separate real drop-off from customers simply having less time.

The funnel shows where performance changed, but not why. Before committing the squad, we need to check timing, processing delays and customer mix. This tells us whether the journey is truly broken or the result only looks worse because the customers or timing changed.

What the data shows
The largest drop is between approval and first deposit.

The decline is 17.6 percentage points, compared with 9.0 points at KYC submission and 2.1 points at approval. That makes the step after approval the first place to investigate, but it does not prove that the problem starts there.

Case clock

The 14-day clock starts at registration.

When approval happens later, customers have fewer days left to make a deposit. The approval-to-deposit rate can therefore fall even when customer behavior after approval has not changed.

Another explanation

Did the customer mix change?

We should compare partners, campaigns, what customers were told when they signed up and how ready they were to invest. More low-intent customers could weaken the funnel even if the product is working as designed.

Decision principle

Move to an earlier step if timing explains the drop.

If approval has slowed but conversion stays stable when customers get the same amount of time after approval, the investigation should move earlier in the journey before we assign the squad.

03 · Partner

Ajaib and the partner need one view of the problem, with a clear owner for every next action.

I would open one joint incident, compare both sides’ data early and escalate delays instead of letting the teams debate ownership while customers remain affected.

Diagnose the correct partnership model

Case fact

The case says that partners collect the initial KYC after account binding and pass the data to Ajaib. What changes is who opens the account. A flow where Ajaib collects the initial KYC would be a new idea, not something the case says already exists.

MODEL A · PARTNER OPENSPartner collects → Ajaib processes → partner opensAjaib completes the additional KYC and returns the information the partner needs to open the account.
MODEL B · AJAIB OPENSPartner collects → Ajaib processes → Ajaib opensAccount readiness, failure ownership and the customer handoff may therefore differ from Model A.

Because these two operating models can fail in different places, we should segment them first and diagnose each journey separately.
